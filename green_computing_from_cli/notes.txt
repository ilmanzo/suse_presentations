  1. Kepler (Kubernetes-based Efficient Power Level Exporter)
  This is currently the "gold standard" for cloud-native power profiling.
   * The Problem it Solves: In the cloud (AWS/GCP), you usually don't have access to Intel RAPL
     because the hypervisor hides it.
   * How it works: It uses eBPF (extended Berkeley Packet Filter) to look at CPU instructions, cache
     misses, and context switches at the kernel level. It then uses a Machine Learning model to
     estimate the power consumption of a specific Pod or Container based on those signals.
   * Why it's cool: It allows you to see the "Joules per Request" for a specific microservice even
     when running on shared hardware in the cloud.


  2. Scaphandre
  A specialized "metrology" agent written in Rust.
   * Focus: It acts as a bridge between the low-level hardware (RAPL, NVIDIA GPUs) and your
     monitoring stack (Prometheus/Grafana).
   * Unique Feature: It can calculate the embodied carbon of the hardware (the energy it took to make
     the server), not just the energy it takes to run your code.


  3. OpenTelemetry (OTel) Semantic Conventions for Energy
  Power measurement is finally becoming a "first-class citizen" in the world of SRE.
   * The Tech: OpenTelemetry is standardizing how energy metrics are named and exported.
   * The Benefit: Soon, you won't need specialized "power tools." Your standard APM dashboard (like
     Datadog or Jaeger) will show energy consumption alongside latency and error rates because the
     metrics follow a universal standard.


  4. Carbon Aware SDK (Green Software Foundation)
  This isn't for measuring, but for acting on the measurements.
   * The Concept: "Spatial and Temporal Shifting."
   * The Tech: A library that tells your program the current "Carbon Intensity" of the grid.
   * Example for your talk: "If my measuring tool tells me this batch job takes 1,000 Joules, the SDK
     can tell the program to wait 3 hours to run it when the wind is blowing and the grid is
     'greener,' effectively reducing the CO2 impact without changing the code."


  How to use this in your 25m Talk:
  I recommend adding a "The Cloud Challenge" slide right after your Python vs. Rust comparison:
   1. Acknowledge the Gap: "RAPL is great for my laptop, but what about my EKS cluster in AWS?"
   2. Introduce Kepler: Explain how eBPF + ML models allow us to 'guess' the power consumption when
      the hardware is hidden.
   3. The Vision: Show a Grafana dashboard where 'Energy' is just another line next to 'CPU' and
      'RAM'.
